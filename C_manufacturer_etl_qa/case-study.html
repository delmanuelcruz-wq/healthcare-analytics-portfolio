1. The business question

Under data agreements, an infusion provider sends manufacturers weekly files about specific products: inventory (stock in and out), dispense (what was actually administered) and, for some programs, treatment status. The files must be right, provably complete, and actually received. A file existing on disk proves none of that.

2. Fictional business context

Two fictional programs: PROGRAM_A covers one infused product (THERAPY_D). PROGRAM_B covers two injected products (THERAPY_F and THERAPY_G) and adds a status file. Eight sites, 24 reporting weeks (May 4 - Oct 18, 2026), 120 logged submissions. The submission log records what the operator said happened: counts, control totals, transfer attempts, acknowledgments.

10. What the synthetic data shows

  • Recomputation catches what the operator log hides. 118 of 120 logged batches match a fresh recomputation. The other two were diagnosed automatically: one file built from the previous week's date range, and one with two duplicate rows left in it.
  • Control totals: two batches counted the header row. One was rejected by the (simulated) manufacturer.
  • Inventory: 573 of 576 site-product-weeks reconcile. The three that do not each carry a count adjustment the ledger never posted. One receipt recorded in cartons reconciles once converted (+18 vials).
  • Status rules engine: of 18,897 status events, 10 escalated (backward moves without approval; treatment continuing after an order was archived as discontinued), 13 sent for review (duplicates, "discontinued" with no dose, an order dated after its first dose), and 3 approved corrections passed with an audit trail.
  • Timing is the biggest real-world gap. 29 of 48 dispense files missed visits completed in the source after the file was built. Files built 2-3 business days after week end captured 92.8-94.3% of visits. Waiting 5 days captures 95.9%, and 8 days 99.6%. Recommendation: build on business day 5 and schedule a restatement for the remainder.
  • History matters: 509 of 614 Program B rows were a second-or-later dose. A week-only extract would have labelled every one of them dose #1.

11. Limitations and next steps

  • Layouts are invented. Real agreements specify fields, codes and formats that must be followed exactly.
  • Transfer and acknowledgment are simulated from the log. A real build would read transfer receipts and manufacturer responses.
  • The rules engine is deliberately conservative: it routes ambiguous evidence to people and never invents a status.
  • Next: automated restatement files, and a per-program layout validator driven by a spec file.
