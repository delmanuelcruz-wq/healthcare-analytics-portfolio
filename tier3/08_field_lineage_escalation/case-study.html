Situation

Users of a delivery explore (specialty-pharmacy shipments to infusion sites) reported that Therapy, Patient and Visit date were blank on most rows. The obvious guess was that the pharmacy feed had stopped sending them.

What went wrong

Nothing was missing from the source. Every blank field reached the explore through one join: delivery_tickets.fill_ref = prescriptions.rx_key. The pharmacy types an original fill as RX0012 and refill n as RX0012-R3. Refills never matched, so 317 of 446 rows (71%) lost every field behind that join.

    T -- "corrected: parse to rx_key + fill_number" --> F[rx_fills]
    F --> RX

    F --> FD["Fill date, Vials"]
    A --> V["Visit date, Visit site"]

Investigation

  1. Field lineage: traced each blank field back to its source column and the join it depends on. All of them shared the same join.
  2. Null-coverage audit: measured the share of rows with a null value for each field in the explore as modeled (outputs/null_coverage.csv). The rate was about the same in every full month (69-73%), so this was a modeling gap from day one, not a recent feed break.
  3. Rejected the quick fix: LIKE rx_key || '%' plus a join on rx_key alone fills the blanks by attaching every fill to every ticket: 1,329 rows for 446 tickets (3.0x), with wrong visit dates (sql/02_quick_fix.sql).
  4. Corrected join: parse the reference, then join the fill on rx_key + fill_number. That gives exactly one row per ticket (sql/03_corrected.sql).

After the model fix, the remaining problems are source issues for the pharmacy-feed owner (outputs/source_issues.csv):

  • 3 fill references that match no fill even after parsing (RXO073-R1 with a letter O, and RX0086-2R with the suffix typed backwards).
  • 4 tickets marked Delivered with no delivered time.
  • 2 tickets shipped to a different site than the visit. This only became visible once the join worked.

Three more blank delivered times are not issues: those shipments were still in transit at the snapshot.

Lessons

  • A blank field is a question about lineage before it is a question about the source.
  • Measure null coverage per field before and after a fix. Whatever survives the fix is the real data-quality list.
  • Reject fixes that remove nulls by multiplying rows. Check row counts against the grain of the base table.
  • Escalate with reproduction steps, the expected-vs-actual table, the root cause and a tested fix, and split model issues from source issues by owner.
